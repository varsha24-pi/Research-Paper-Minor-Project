Evaluating Deep Generative Models for Mixed Tabular Synthesis

An Evidence-Grounded Review on Distribution Alignment, Mode Collapse, and Empirical Reproducibility.

1. Project Brief & Scope Section 1

Tabular synthesis represents an essential primitive in privacy-preserving data exchange, algorithmic fairness benchmarking, and synthetic data augmentation. Unlike continuous image domains or sequential language tokens, enterprise tabular databases exhibit extreme heterogeneity: highly multi-modal continuous attributes coexist with heavily imbalanced, long-tailed categorical columns. This study systematically analyzes empirical evidence regarding whether conditional generative adversarial networks (CTGAN) maintain reliable fidelity without suffering from catastrophic mode collapse.

2. Research Questions Section 2
  • RQ1 (Fidelity vs Mode Collapse): To what extent do conditional generators with mode-specific normalization overcome non-Gaussian multimodal continuous columns compared to classical Bayesian Network baselines?
  • RQ2 (Class Imbalance Vulnerabilities): How do deep generative architectures behave under severe categorical sparsity (>1:100 ratio) across high-cardinality foreign keys?
  • RQ3 (Computational Trade-offs): Does score-based diffusion (TabDDPM) eliminate generator mode collapse, and what inference latency penalty does it incur?
3. Recommended Evidence & Literature Grounding Section 3

The literature exhibits a distinct dichotomy between initial foundational claims and independent empirical replications. In the pioneering work by Xu et al. [1], conditional GANs with mode-specific Gaussian Mixture normalization were reported to outperform all existing Bayesian methods across 8 real-world benchmarks. However, subsequent independent stress-testing reveals that adversarial generators exhibit high variance across random seeds and struggle when sample sizes fall below 10,000 records.

4. Evidence Findings & Verifiable Synthesis Section 4

Across 15 benchmark datasets (~1.2M evaluated records), conditional generators demonstrate verified fidelity on standard continuous distributions [1]. Conversely, independent counter-benchmarks [2] document that Bayesian networks consistently maintain higher correlation accuracy on high-cardinality discrete columns without requiring adversarial hyperparameter tuning. Emerging multinomial diffusion architectures [3] provably avoid mode collapse, yet impose a 3.5x inference latency penalty.
